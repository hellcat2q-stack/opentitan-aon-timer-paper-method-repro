B. Compared Methods and Metrics
Our method, denoted as Full, combines continuous refill,
cluster-level UCB, sequence-level UCB, and runtime-aware
scoring. We compare it with Random Selection and ablations
that remove individual scheduling components. Accumulated
runtime is the sum of simulator runtimes across completed
runs, whereas wall time is the end-to-end elapsed time in-
cluding parallel execution, coverage-report generation, and
scheduling overhead. We report final coverage, accumulated
runtime, and wall time.

C. Ablation Analysis
In the AON Timer experiment, the complete two-level
scheduler outperforms both single-level ablations. Sequence
UCBOnlyreaches52.15%,indicatingthatsequencefeedback
helps, but it remains 23.18 percentage points below Full
because it lacks explicit target-cluster selection. Cluster UCB
Only reaches 27.17% and consumes substantially higher run-
time, indicating that cluster prioritization alone is insufficient
in this experiment.
Continuous refill mainly improves wall-clock time rather
thanfinalcoverage.FullandNoIncrementalRefillbothreach
75.33% final coverage, but Full reduces wall-clock time from
6160.74s to 4764.13s, a 22.7% reduction. This is consistent
with the motivation of asynchronous refill: when simulations
have heterogeneous runtimes, waiting for an entire batch
delaystheuseoffeedbackfromfastertestsandleavesworker
slots underutilized.
Runtime-awarescoringprovidesanadditionalefficiencyim-
provement. Full and No Runtime Penalty reach the same final
coverage of 75.33%, but Full reduces wall-clock time from
4943.13s to 4764.13s, a 3.6% reduction. This is consistent
with the runtime-aware score in Section IV-B: long-running
sequencescanstillbeselected,buttheymustprovidestronger
expected coverage value.
